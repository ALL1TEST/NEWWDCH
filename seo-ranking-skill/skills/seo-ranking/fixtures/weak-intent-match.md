# The History of Running Shoes

People who set out to buy running shoes online rarely pause to think about where the shoe under their feet came from, yet the modern trainer is the end of a long line of experiments. Early competitive runners raced in plain leather shoes with steel spikes screwed into the soles — heavy when dry, punishing when wet, and usually gone by the end of a hard season.

The interwar years brought lighter canvas designs, and after the Second World War a small Japanese firm began gluing rubber soles onto court shoes — the quiet beginning of a giant. One famous story from the 1960s credits an American track coach with pouring urethane rubber into a household waffle iron in search of a grippier tread; the waffle pattern eventually reached the road and stayed there for decades.

Cushioning then took over the story: foam midsoles, air chambers, and gel pockets each enjoyed their decade of dominance, argued over by runners and engineers alike. The last twenty years changed the question from how much cushioning a shoe holds to how much energy it returns, and carbon-fibre plates inside supercritical foam let racers hold the same pace for less effort.

Whether you lace up a vintage-styled trainer or a plated racer, every stride stands on more than a century of trial and error.
