## Why Succulents Drop Their Leaves

Leaf drop is the fastest way a succulent tells you that something is wrong, and the pattern of the drop usually identifies the cause. Leaves that turn soft, translucent, and yellow before they fall point to overwatering, by far the most common cause: succulents store water in their leaves, and soil that stays wet leaves them nowhere to put more. Water less often, and move the plant into a faster-draining mix if the old soil stays damp for days after watering.

## Sudden Light and Temperature Changes

Leaves that shrivel dry and drop evenly across the plant usually follow a sudden change of place — a shade-grown succulent moved into harsh afternoon sun, or a cold draft beside a winter window. Introduce plants to a brighter or colder spot gradually over a week or two instead of all at once.

## Natural Aging

Finally, a healthy succulent slowly reabsorbs its oldest, lowest leaves and sheds a dry, papery one from time to time. That is ordinary growth, not a problem to fix, and it is a sign the plant is funding new growth at the top.
