# How to Clean a Coffee Maker

A good cup of coffee is part of many morning routines. For most people, the day simply starts better when the coffee tastes the way it should. Over time, though, coffee makers collect oils and mineral deposits that quietly change how a brew tastes, and cleaning the machine is a task that gets postponed until the flavor drifts. Descaling a drip machine every one to three months, depending on your water hardness, keeps the flavor where it belongs.

## What You'll Need

- White vinegar
- Fresh, cool water
- One paper filter
- Mild dish soap and a soft brush for the removable parts

## Steps

1. Empty the carafe and remove the used grounds and filter from the basket.
2. Fill the reservoir with a half-and-half mix of white vinegar and water.
3. Place the paper filter in the basket.
4. Run a full brew cycle.
5. Pour the hot vinegar mixture out of the carafe when the cycle finishes.
6. Fill the reservoir with fresh water.
7. Run another full cycle.
8. Run one more fresh-water cycle so no vinegar smell remains in the brew.
9. Wash the carafe and the basket with the dish soap and soft brush.
10. Dry both parts and return them to the machine.

## Why Regular Cleaning Matters

Coffee leaves behind two kinds of buildup, and each one works against flavor in its own way. Coffee oils are sticky, and they gradually coat the brew basket, the carafe, and the internal paths the water travels through the machine. Those oils turn rancid over weeks and months, and they lend stale or bitter notes to otherwise fresh beans. Mineral scale is the second problem, and it comes from the water rather than the coffee. Heated water deposits calcium and magnesium on the heating element and inside the tubes, and a heavily scaled machine has to work harder to reach brewing temperature. A machine that runs cooler than intended under-extracts the grounds, which shows up in the cup as sour and thin rather than rich. Damp reservoirs also collect the ordinary grime that accumulates anywhere water sits in a kitchen. The vinegar in the steps above dissolves the mineral scale, while the soap-and-water wash handles the oils on the parts you can reach.

## When to Clean Again

Mark the calendar rather than trusting memory: descale monthly with hard tap water, or roughly quarterly with filtered or softened water, adjusting as your own brew's taste drifts. The carafe and basket take a weekly wash, which takes less time than the vinegar cycle and keeps the oils from building back up.
