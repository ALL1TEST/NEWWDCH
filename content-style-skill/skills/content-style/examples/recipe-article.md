# Skillet Chicken Thighs with Garlic-Herb Butter

Chicken thighs have the two things weeknight cooking wants: skin with enough fat to render into something crisp, and meat forgiving enough to stay juicy a minute past your best attention. This version finishes in one skillet in about 35 minutes, with a garlic-herb butter that turns the pan drippings into a sauce you spoon over the top. A heavy pan and an instant-read thermometer are the only real equipment needs — poultry is done at an internal 165°F (74°C) at the thickest part, and a thermometer removes the guessing.

## Ingredients

Serves 4.

- 6 bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs (about 2 lb / 900 g)
- 2 teaspoons kosher salt
- 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
- 1 tablespoon neutral oil (avocado, grapeseed, or canola)
- 4 tablespoons unsalted butter (about 55 g)
- 4 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- 1 tablespoon fresh lemon juice (about half a lemon)
- 2 tablespoons chopped fresh parsley, plus a few whole sprigs for the pan

Substitutions: chives or dill stand in for the parsley; ghee works in place of the butter and takes the pan a little hotter before browning. Whole thyme or rosemary sprigs can replace the parsley sprigs in the pan.

## Instructions

1. Pat the chicken thighs dry on both sides with paper towels.
2. Season the thighs all over with the salt and pepper.
3. Set a heavy skillet — cast iron or stainless steel — over medium heat for 2 minutes.
4. Add the oil and swirl it to coat the pan.
5. Lay the thighs in the pan skin-side down, placing the farthest pieces first.
6. Cook undisturbed for 7 to 9 minutes, until the skin is deep golden and releases from the pan on its own.
7. Flip the thighs and cook 5 to 6 minutes more, until a thermometer in the thickest part reads 165°F (74°C).
8. Move the thighs to a plate, skin-side up.
9. Spoon out all but a thin film of fat from the pan.
10. Set the pan back over low heat and add the butter, garlic, and herb sprigs.
11. Cook, swirling, for 30 to 60 seconds, until the garlic smells fragrant but has not browned.
12. Turn off the heat and stir the lemon juice into the butter.
13. Return the thighs to the pan, skin-side up.
14. Spoon the garlic-herb butter over the skin and serve.

## Tips

- Dry skin is the whole game. Paper towels before cooking, and a little air time after salting if you have it, are what separate crisp from steamed.
- If the skin grips the pan when you go to flip, it is not ready. Rendered skin releases on its own; give it another minute rather than forcing it.
- Save whatever butter is left in the pan. It keeps a day or two in the fridge and improves almost any vegetable you reheat in it.

## Storage and Make-Ahead

Leftovers keep in an airtight container in the fridge for about 3 to 4 days. To reheat without wrecking the skin, use a skillet over medium heat, skin-side up and uncovered, for a few minutes rather than the microwave, which softens everything. The thighs also freeze reasonably well — expect up to about 3 months at normal freezer temperatures, with the skin a little less crisp after thawing. Thaw overnight in the fridge before reheating.
