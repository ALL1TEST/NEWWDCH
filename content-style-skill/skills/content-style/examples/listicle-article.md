# 9 Indoor Plants That Tolerate Low Light

No houseplant survives in zero light, and that fact makes most "low light plants" lists dishonest by omission. "Tolerate" here has a precise meaning: the plant stays healthy and presentable in a north-facing window, or a few feet back from a brighter one — the spots where most houseplants slowly fall apart. These nine were selected for that trait, plus a second filter: they also forgive irregular watering, because dim corners and busy weeks tend to arrive together.

> **Warning:** If you share the house with a cat or dog, note that several plants on this list — pothos, heartleaf philodendron, arrowhead plant, peace lily, snake plant, and ZZ plant among them — are commonly listed as toxic to pets if chewed. Keep them out of reach or choose around them.

Each entry follows the same shape: what the plant is, who it suits, and the detail worth remembering it by.

## 1. ZZ Plant (Zamioculcas zamiifolia)

Glossy, dark-green leaflets on arching stems, growing from fat water-storing rhizomes. It suits the lowest-maintenance spot in the house — dim hallways, offices, a first plant for a first-time owner. The standout: those rhizomes let it commonly go two to three weeks between waterings, and a bit longer in winter.

## 2. Snake Plant (Dracaena trifasciata)

Upright, sword-shaped leaves with sharp variegation, architectural enough to read as furniture. It suits modern rooms and anyone who wants a plant that looks deliberate rather than leafy. The standout: it handles both dim corners and weeks of neglect with the same flat expression, and it stays put size-wise for years.

## 3. Pothos (Epipremnum aureum)

A trailing vine with heart-shaped leaves that forgives nearly everything. It suits shelves, the tops of cabinets, and hanging pots where a fuller plant would crowd the light. The standout: a leggy pothos cut back and stuck in water regrows roots in a couple of weeks, which makes it the cheapest plant to multiply.

## 4. Cast Iron Plant (Aspidistra elatior)

Deep-green, paddle-shaped leaves on short stems, slow and unbothered by nature. It suits the genuinely dim spots — interior hallways, north-facing landings — where everything else thins out. The standout is the reputation encoded in its common name: it shrugs off irregular watering, cool drafts, and general neglect, at the price of growing slowly.

## 5. Chinese Evergreen (Aglaonema)

Broad leaves patterned in silver, green, or red-tinged cultivars, depending on the variety. It suits living rooms and bedrooms that get modest filtered light and want some color from foliage rather than flowers. The standout: the patterned foliage stays vivid in conditions that wash the color out of fussier patterned plants.

## 6. Peace Lily (Spathiphyllum)

Dark leaves with white spathes, tidy and naturally rounded. It suits a slightly humid room — a bathroom with any window at all is ideal — and an owner who can water on something closer to a schedule. The standout: it droops theatrically when dry and recovers within hours of watering, which makes it the most communicative plant on this list. Flowers, where they appear, come from giving it the brightest spot it tolerates.

## 7. Parlor Palm (Chamaedorea elegans)

A small, soft-fronded palm with a Victorian drawing-room history of tolerating dim interiors. It suits corners and side tables where a feathery texture is wanted without bulk. The standout: unlike most palms, which sulk indoors, this one was essentially domesticated for low light a century before "low light" was a marketing category.

## 8. Heartleaf Philodendron (Philodendron hederaceum)

Fast, vining, and glossy, with leaves that stay small and neat in dim conditions. It suits bookcases and desk edges where a plant can spill without taking over. The standout: it grows visibly even in a dim apartment through winter, when most of this list merely holds steady.

## 9. Arrowhead Plant (Syngonium podophyllum)

A bushy trailer whose leaves shift from arrow-shaped to lobed as the plant matures. It suits kitchens and bathrooms with filtered light and anyone who wants a full-looking basket without a trailing habit. The standout: it responds to a brighter window with visibly larger leaves within weeks, so it tells you directly when it wants more light.

## Quick Picks

Play it entirely safe: ZZ plant. Dark corner plus total neglect: snake plant. Something trailing for a shelf: pothos. The dimmest genuine spot in the house: cast iron plant. A bathroom with a small window: peace lily. Whichever you choose, water by the finger test — two inches into the soil, dry before the next drink — and rotate the pot into a brighter window for a week every month or so. Tolerating low light is not the same as preferring it, and these plants will quietly say so.
